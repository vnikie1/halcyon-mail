Halcyon Mail
A desktop email client for Windows 11

Privacy policy

Version 1.4 · Last updated 25 November 2026

Halcyon is a mail client that runs on your computer. It has no server, no account, and no business model that involves knowing anything about you.

This document says what leaves your machine and what does not. Every claim in it is checkable against the source code, which is published for that reason.

Nothing about you or your mail is sent to the makers of this application, ever. There is nowhere for it to go. No analytics, no usage statistics, no crash reporting service, no telemetry of any description.

Your mail goes to and from your own email provider, over an encrypted connection, exactly as it would with any other mail client.

What Halcyon connects to, and why

Connection When What it carries
Your email provider’s IMAP server While the app is running Your username, your password or access token, and your mail
Your email provider’s SMTP server When you send The message and its recipients
Your provider’s sign-in page, in your own browser When you add an account that uses OAuth Whatever your provider’s sign-in requires
Autoconfiguration records for your email domain Once, while adding an account, to find the server settings Your email domain — example.com, not your address
github.com When you open Settings → General, or press Check for updates. Never from the Microsoft Store version Nothing about you. A request for a small public file
Servers named in the messages you read Only if you allow images to load See Remote images

There are no other connections. Nothing runs on a schedule except your own mail sync.

What is stored, and where

All of it is on your computer. None of it is uploaded.

Your mail, its attachments, the search index, and what the junk filter has learned %LOCALAPPDATA%\com.uniki.halcyon
Passwords and OAuth tokens Windows Credential Manager, protected by your Windows account
Logs, and crash reports if the app ever stops unexpectedly %LOCALAPPDATA%\com.uniki.halcyon\diagnostics
Window size, theme, and your settings %APPDATA%\com.uniki.halcyon

Passwords are never written to the database, to a configuration file, to a log, or into an error message. They are handed to Windows Credential Manager and referenced by a name that is not itself a secret. There is an automated test that fails the build if any error type in the application is capable of printing one.

The mail database is not encrypted. Anything running as you can read it, which is equally true of every desktop mail client. BitLocker — Windows’ full-disk encryption — is what protects it if your computer is lost or stolen, and turning it on is worthwhile.

Google accounts

Adding a Google account asks Google for one permission, which Google’s own screen describes as “Read, compose, send and permanently delete all your email from Gmail” — the scope https://mail.google.com/. Halcyon asks for that one and no other, because it is the only one Google’s IMAP and SMTP servers accept.

With an app password instead. A Google account can also be added with an app password, which you create in your Google Account and paste into Halcyon. That way nothing is asked of Google on Halcyon’s behalf: there is no permission screen, and the app password is what lets Halcyon reach your mail. What Halcyon accesses, how it uses it, who it is shared with and how long it is kept are the same either way, and are set out below.

What it accesses. Your Gmail messages — their headers, bodies and attachments — the names of your labels and mailboxes, each message’s flags (read, starred, and so on), and your account’s email address. Nothing else in your Google Account: not your contacts, calendar, files or profile.

How it uses it. For what a mail client does, at your direction: downloading your mail to your computer so that you can read and search it, sending what you write, and moving, flagging and deleting messages when you ask. The junk filter also learns from the messages you mark, as described below.

Who it is shared with. No one. What Halcyon receives from Google is stored only on your computer, in the places listed above; mail you send goes to Google’s mail server and on to the recipients you chose. Halcyon does not send Google user data to us or to anyone else — none of it reaches us, so no one here can read it — and does not sell it or use it for advertising.

How it is protected. Every connection to Google is encrypted. The sign-in token, or the app password, is held by Windows Credential Manager, protected by your Windows account, and is never written to the database, a file or a log. The mail database itself is not encrypted, as described above.

How long it is kept, and how it is deleted. Your mail stays on your computer for as long as the account is in Halcyon, and follows what is on Gmail: a message deleted there is deleted here at the next sync. Removing the account (Settings → Accounts) deletes that account’s stored mail, its search entries and its sign-in token or app password from your computer. Uninstalling the Microsoft Store version removes the data Windows keeps for it; removing your accounts first, or deleting the folders listed above, makes certain nothing is left. You can also withdraw Halcyon’s access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which stops the stored token working, or, for an account added with an app password, by deleting that password at myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.

Machine learning. Halcyon’s junk filter is a small statistical classifier. It learns only from the messages you mark as junk or not junk, it runs on your computer, and it is used only to sort your own mail. What it learns — counts of words — is kept in the mail database on your computer, is never sent anywhere, and is deleted with Halcyon’s data. Halcyon does not use Google user data to develop, improve or train any other artificial-intelligence or machine-learning model, and sends none of it to any AI service.

Halcyon’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Remote images

Many messages contain images loaded from the sender’s server. Requesting one tells that sender you opened the message, roughly when, and the network address you opened it from. This is the read receipt nobody agreed to, and it is how commercial mail tracks you.

Halcyon loads remote images automatically by default, because a mail client that shows broken pictures is one people stop using. This is the one default in the application chosen against the security advice, and it is a setting rather than a decision made for you: Settings → Privacy → “Show images in messages automatically.” Turning it off shows a banner on each message instead, and images load only when you ask.

Whatever the setting, message content is stripped of scripts and displayed in a sandbox that cannot run code or reach your files.

Crash reports

If Halcyon stops unexpectedly it writes a file describing what it was doing — the error and the sequence of functions that led to it. That file stays on your computer.

It is never uploaded. There is no server to upload it to. You can read the reports under Settings → Advanced, open the folder, delete them, or ignore them, in which case old ones are eventually discarded automatically.

If you choose to send one to us to help with a problem, you do so yourself, deliberately, by attaching the file — and you can read exactly what is in it first.

Updates

Opening Settings → General, where the Updates section is, asks GitHub once whether there is a newer version, and so does pressing Check for updates. Each is one request for one small public file. Your address is visible to GitHub, as it is to any web server you contact; nothing identifying you, your accounts or your mail is sent, and nothing checks at any other time — not at start-up, and not on a timer. The Microsoft Store version does not do this at all: the updater is not in it, and the Store handles its own updates.

For Store installs, Microsoft gives us aggregate install counts, ratings and crash figures. That is Microsoft measuring their own platform rather than this application reporting on you, and it cannot be turned off from here — but you should not have to discover it, so it is written down.

Children

Halcyon is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone, of any age.

Your rights

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to disclose, correct, export or delete. Deleting your data means removing your accounts in Halcyon or deleting the folders listed above; the downloadable installer’s uninstaller offers to do it for you.

Mail held by your email provider is governed by that provider’s own privacy policy, not this one.

Changes to this policy

Any change appears here and in the application’s changelog, both of which are public and have a full history. The version and date at the top say which one you are reading.
